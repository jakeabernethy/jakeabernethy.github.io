Longer academic bio

Jacob Abernethy is a Research Scientist at Google, where he is part of the ML Theory Team and has worked closely with Gemini’s Large-Scale Pretraining team. He is also an Associate Professor in the School of Computer Science at Georgia Tech, currently on leave, where he continues to advise PhD students and postdocs.

His academic work sits at the intersection of machine learning, optimization, statistics, economics, and game theory, with a long-running focus on online learning, learning in games, market design, and the dynamics of decision-making systems. He completed his PhD in Computer Science at UC Berkeley, advised by Peter Bartlett, and then spent two years as a Simons Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Pennsylvania working with Michael Kearns.

Before Georgia Tech, he was on the faculty at the University of Michigan. Across these roles, his work has ranged from theoretical foundations of learning and optimization to public-interest data science and large-scale AI systems.

Water safety, Flint, and BlueConduit

During the Flint water crisis, Jacob collaborated with Eric Schwartz and students from the Michigan Data Science Team on statistical tools for identifying homes likely to have hazardous lead service lines. The goal was practical: help replacement crews prioritize digs and reduce wasted excavations.

That work led to KDD papers including ActiveRemediation: The Search for Lead Pipes in Flint, Michigan, and eventually grew into BlueConduit, a software company focused on helping cities and utilities inventory and replace risky water infrastructure.
